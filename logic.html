Logic Pro X

The shortcuts, tools, and features that actually make music

Logic Pro is deep, and most of its speed lives in the keyboard. This is the practical reference — the commands worth burning into muscle memory, the toolbox, the window laid out, and the features that take you from a blank project to a finished track. It pairs with the Learn Production series: those teach the craft, this is the machine.

① Know Your Cockpit

The Main Window

Everything hangs off one window. The Control Bar up top drives transport and toggles the panels; the Tracks Area in the center is where you arrange; and the panels tuck in and out with single keys so the screen stays yours.

③ Point, Cut, Shape

The Toolbox

Press T (or right-click) to pop the Tool menu at the pointer. Logic holds two tools at once — a left-click tool and a Command-click tool — so you can flip between, say, Pointer and Scissors without a trip to the menu.

Pointer

Select, move, resize, and loop regions. Your default — the one you return to.

Pencil

Draw in notes, automation points, or a new empty region. Create from nothing.

Scissors

Split a region at the click point. Cut a loop into pieces, chop a vocal.

Glue

Join selected regions into one. The undo of Scissors.

Eraser

Delete what you click. Fast cleanup of stray notes or regions.

Marquee

Select any time range across any tracks — then cut, move, or delete that exact slice. One of Logic's most powerful edits.

Solo

Click-hold a region to hear it alone while the playhead runs through it. Audition in place.

Flex

Grab and drag audio in time without changing pitch — tighten a lazy note, fix timing by hand.

Fade

Drag fade-ins/outs and crossfades on audio edges. Smooth every seam.

④ What Actually Makes Music

Features for Making Music

Beyond recording and editing, these are the engines that turn a sketch into a song — the parts of Logic that generate, fix, and perform for you.

Software Instruments

Synths, sampled pianos, strings, drums — play them with Musical Typing (⌘+K) or a MIDI keyboard. Alchemy, Sampler, and Vintage keys are studios in themselves.

Drummer

A virtual session drummer that plays realistic grooves to your song — pick a style & personality, dial complexity/loudness, and it follows your arrangement. Instant, human-feeling drums.

Apple Loops

Thousands of royalty-free loops (O) that auto-match your tempo & key. Drag one in and it just fits — sketch a whole idea in minutes.

Quantize

Snap notes to the grid to tighten timing — set the value in the Region inspector. Use Q-Strength to tighten partway so it stays human, not robotic.

Flex Time

Stretch and nudge audio timing like MIDI — quantize a sloppy guitar take, tighten a drum performance, or align two tracks. No re-recording.

Flex Pitch

Tune vocals and monophonic audio note-by-note — drag a flat note into place, straighten drift, reshape vibrato. Logic's built-in pitch correction.

Smart Tempo

Logic detects the tempo of a performance and bends the project grid to match — or bends the audio to the grid. Record without a click and stay in time.

Automation

Draw or record changes over time (A) — volume swells, filter sweeps, pan moves, any plugin knob. The mix that moves with the song.

Take Folders & Comping

Record several passes into one folder, then Quick Swipe the best bits into a flawless comp. Detailed in the Recording Guide.

Track Stacks

Fold many tracks into one — a Summing Stack (⌘+⇧+D) to process a group as one, or a Folder Stack to tidy. Big sessions stay sane.

Live Loops

A grid of clips you trigger and layer in real time (like Ableton's session view) — jam an arrangement by launching cells, then record the performance into the timeline.

Bounce & Stems

Export the finished mix (⌘+B) to WAV/MP3, or bounce each track as a stem for collaborators. The final step.

⑤ The Most Important Setting Nobody Explains

Buffer Size & Latency

Your computer can't process audio one sample at a time — it would never keep up. Instead the interface collects a small batch of samples, hands the whole batch to the CPU to process, and moves on. That batch size is the I/O Buffer Size, measured in samples (32, 64, 128 … 1024). It's the single most important performance setting in Logic — and it's a trade-off with no universally "right" value. You change it depending on what you're doing.

Small buffer = play in real time; large buffer = run heavy sessions without glitches. Slide it to the job.

Latency vs. CPU, in one table

Small (32–128) — low latency, high CPU loadrecord
Large (512–1024) — high latency, rock-solid & CPU headroommix / master

Latency is the delay between playing a note and hearing it back. Bigger buffer = bigger delay (128 samples ≈ 3 ms, 1024 ≈ 21 ms). That delay only matters when you're recording live and monitoring through Logic — you don't want to hear yourself lag. So you record on a small buffer.

But a small buffer makes the CPU process tiny batches thousands of times a second — pile on plugins and it can't keep up, and you get crackles or a dreaded "System Overload" stop. When you're not recording — mixing, and especially mastering — there's no live input, so latency is harmless. Crank the buffer up to give heavy plugins the room to run clean.

Why It Matters for Mastering

Mastering chains are CPU-hungry — linear-phase EQ, look-ahead & true-peak limiters, multiband, oversampling. With nothing to record, set the buffer to 512 or 1024 so playback never glitches while those heavy processors do their work.

The Bounce Doesn't Care

Buffer size only affects real-time monitoring. Your final bounce is rendered offline at full quality no matter the setting — so a big buffer costs you nothing in the exported master. Latency never reaches the file.

Sample Rate ≠ Buffer Size

Two different things. Sample rate (44.1/48 kHz) is the audio's resolution, set per project. Buffer size is a live-performance setting you change freely — it doesn't alter your audio, just how smoothly Logic plays it back.

IN LOGIC PRO Set it in Settings ▸ Audio ▸ Devices ▸ I/O Buffer Size. Logic shows a Resulting/Roundtrip Latency readout right below so you can see the ms cost. Record at 64 or 128; mix and master at 512 or 1024. Need to record through a plugin-heavy session? Tap Low Latency Mode in the control bar — it temporarily bypasses high-latency plugins instead of forcing you to lower the buffer. And if you hit System Overload, raise the buffer or Freeze heavy tracks.
⑥ Habits That Compound

Workflow

Start From a Template

Build a session with your usual tracks, routing, and tempo, then Save as Template. Every new song opens ready to work.

Name & Color as You Go

Rename tracks and color regions by section or instrument. A readable project is a fast project.

Save Versions

⌘+S constantly, and use File ▸ Save As at milestones so you can always roll back a bold idea.

Learn Five Keys a Week

Don't memorize the whole list — pick the five actions you repeat most and drill those until they're automatic. Then five more.

Freeze to Save CPU

When a track's plugins get heavy, Freeze it — Logic renders it temporarily so it costs almost nothing, un-freeze to edit again.

Use the Arrangement Track

Block the song into named sections you can drag — the whole method is in the Arrangement Guide.

The gear rewards fluency: the less you think about where a command is, the more you think about the music. Learn the machine so you can forget it.

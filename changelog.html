Changelog

What's New, As It Happened

Everything shipped here, newest first. No version numbers — just the work, in the order it happened. RSS feed ⟶

🌿 August 21, 2026 🌿

The Synth now wires itself. Click Osc and you have a working instrument — an Output appears and the cable is drawn for you. Everything you add after that joins the signal path on its own: sources stack in parallel at the front, effects land at the end where they belong. Delete a module and the chain closes over the gap instead of going silent. A modular synth is a wonderful thing to understand and a miserable thing to be handed cold, and dragging four cables before you hear a note was the wrong first five minutes.

Because the order is now the interesting decision rather than the tedious one, every module in the chain got ◂ ▸ arrows to move itself along the signal path. Filter before delay is a different instrument than delay before filter, and that comparison is now two clicks instead of a re-patch. ⌗ Tidy lays the rack out left-to-right in signal order so the picture matches the sound. Drag a module anywhere you like and the auto-layout backs off for good.

The LFO can finally be pointed at things. Modulation isn't audio, so no cable could ever carry it — which meant until now the only way to get an LFO onto a filter cutoff was to load a preset that already had one. It now has a Drives menu listing every parameter in the rack, in signal order, and picking one sets a depth range that actually suits it: choose Filter · Cutoff and the sweep becomes 200–4000 Hz instead of the ±100 that would have done nothing. A dashed violet line shows what's driving what. Vibrato, wah, tremolo and auto-pan are each one menu pick.

Cables are still real and still yours — tap or drag to build parallel paths and feedback loops the automatic routing won't invent — and a cable you drew by hand is never re-routed by the automatic wiring. Two more fixes along the way: a note held while you delete or reorder a module now re-articulates through the new path instead of dropping to near-silence, and + Channel Strip splices into the rack you already have rather than standing up a second, disconnected one beside it.

The Synth's computer keyboard no longer traps you in one octave. Press Z and X — or the new −/+ buttons beside the octave readout — to move the whole keyboard across the full MIDI range, and C/V to ride velocity. The default layout also grew from an octave to a tenth (A W S E D F T G Y H U J K O L P ;), so full chords and wide voicings fit under your hands.

And because no two keyboard layouts agree, you can now bind your own keys. Click ⌨ Remap, click a piano key, press whatever key should play it — repeat as long as you like, then Esc. Your bindings save in the browser and come back on your next visit; right-click Remap to restore the default. The on-screen keys always show the letter that actually plays them, so the layout is never a guess. Notes held through an octave change now release correctly too — the note you started is the note that stops.

New: a drum machine under the Synth's sequencer. Four programmable voices — kick, snare, hat, clap — on their own grid, running in lock-step with the melodic sequence and sharing its tempo, swing, and length. Click cells to program a pattern, or hit Beat for an instant four-on-the-floor to jam over. Drum hits are scheduled directly on the audio clock rather than the animation frame, so they stay sample-accurate even when the tab is busy, and patterns save and share along with the rest of your patch.

Split the site's music menu in two. Music Tools now holds the seven things you use — Synth, Song Analyzer, Tuner, Tap BPM, Fretboard, Chord Voicings, Songwriter — while Music Education holds the ten things you read: the production, recording, arrangement, effects, mastering and Logic Pro guides, plus theory, the jam guide, ear training and the chord-tone trainer. One seventeen-item dropdown had become a wall; making instruments and lessons separate ideas means you find either one without scanning past the other.

🌿 July 29, 2026 🌿

The Song Analyzer got a much deeper brain. Feed it a YouTube link or your own audio file and it now returns a genuinely comprehensive read: precise BPM with the half/double-time alternative and a 3/4-vs-4/4 meter estimate (waltzes finally get 3-beat bars), the key with a confidence score and closest alternative, the song's structure mapped into sections you can click to jump around, its energy arc drawn over time, and the mix's tonal brightness and dynamic range. YouTube failures now explain themselves too — if a video is age-restricted or bot-checked, it tells you plainly and points you to the file-drop path, which always works.

New: Sound Dispatches — the music counterpart to AI Dispatches. A live, searchable feed pulling from ten sources across audio research (arXiv sound & audio-processing preprints), gear & software (Synthtopia, Bedroom Producers Blog, Gearnews — free plugins turn up constantly), and production analysis (CDM, Attack, MusicTech, MusicRadar, Sound on Sound). Refreshes itself automatically, filterable by category and source — so the music side of this site stays as current as the AI side, with no hand-updating.

Self-hosted the site's fonts. Cinzel, EB Garamond, and JetBrains Mono now load straight from our own server instead of Google's — so pages paint faster (no round-trip to a font CDN, no render-blocking external stylesheet, two fewer domains to connect to) and it's more private: your browser no longer pings Google just to read the site. Same type, ~136 KB, cached hard.

The Synth can now record your jam to a file. Hit Record, play, and hit it again — your performance downloads as an audio file you can keep, post, or drop into a project. Together with shareable patch links, everything you make on the synth now leaves the screen: share the sound, or capture the performance.

You can now share a Synth patch as a link. Hit Share Patch and the entire rack — every module, knob, cable, and hidden modulation — is packed into a URL and copied to your clipboard. Send it to anyone and it rebuilds your exact sound in their browser, ready to play. Sound design stops being something trapped on your screen and becomes something you can pass around.

Made the Synth easier to patch and richer to play. You can now drag a cable straight from one port to another — or keep tapping — and while you're patching, every compatible port glows and the incompatible ones dim, so a wrong connection is basically impossible. Added five new presets built around the new filter — Acid, Sub Bass, Supersaw, Growl, and Brass — bringing the one-click patch library to eighteen. Under the hood, modules now clean up every audio node they create when removed (no more slow resource creep in long sessions), and the built-in guide was updated to teach the new drive, slope, and drag-to-shape envelope.

Brought the AI Evolution timeline up to the present. It now names the 2026 generation turnover — Anthropic's Claude 5 family, OpenAI's GPT-5, Google's Gemini 3 — the arrival of routine million-token context windows, and the moment open-weight models closed to within a few months of the frontier, led mostly by Chinese labs (DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, GLM) while Meta stepped back from open Llama. The model comparisons throughout the page were refreshed to match. (The live news feed already keeps this-week's headlines current on its own, from ten sources.)

Gave the Synth real sound-design teeth. The Filter now has a drive stage — pre-filter saturation that lets high resonance scream instead of thinning out — a selectable slope (−12 / −24 / −48 dB per octave, gentle to brutal), a notch mode, and a wider resonance range that self-oscillates at the top. And the Envelope now draws its ADSR as a live curve you can shape by dragging the handles — attack, decay-into-sustain, release — instead of guessing four sliders; drag the graph or the sliders and the other follows. First step of a deeper synth pass: easier patching, a mod matrix, and save-your-patch next.

🌿 July 8, 2026 🌿

Fixed the typography on four pages — the AI News feed, Chord-Tone Trainer, Chord Voicings, and Ear Training — that were never loading the site's fonts, so they'd been quietly rendering in the browser's default serif instead of Cinzel and EB Garamond. They now match the rest of the site. While there, warmed up the connection to the font-file server across every page so text paints a touch faster on first load.

Tuned the site's caching so repeat visits are near-instant. Every piece of the site's own JavaScript now carries a version stamp, which lets the browser and Cloudflare's edge cache it permanently instead of re-downloading it on every page — so once you've loaded the site, moving between pages and coming back pulls almost nothing over the network. Pinned third-party libraries are cached for a month. Fewer requests to the origin, faster loads, a higher cache-hit rate.

Fixed a quiet but real bug on the Song Analyzer: a naming clash between the page's own audio element and the site's shared script left the shared script dead on that page, so navigating to the analyzer always forced a slow full-page reload instead of an instant transition. The page's script is now properly scoped, so it coexists with the shared one — smoother navigation, and the analyzer is now a first-class citizen of the site's fast page router.

Folded the Music Theory tool's note-playback onto the shared audio-core.js engine — one source of truth for turning a note into a frequency, and a reliable resume-on-tap so the very first chord you play always sounds (some browsers start audio muted until you interact). Same ongoing pass: every interactive feature standing on the same solid foundation.

Smoothed out the Song Analyzer's play-along. The chord and bar highlighting followed the music on a coarse 200 ms timer that re-scanned and re-scrolled the whole chart every tick — now it tracks the audio clock frame-accurately and only touches the page when the current chord, bar, or second actually changes, so the highlight glides instead of stepping. Re-analyzing a song also cleanly cancels the previous request instead of leaving it running in the background. Part of the ongoing pass to make every interactive feature more stable and more fluid.

Made Tap BPM smarter and steadier. Tap detection now uses a sliding window with median-based outlier rejection — an accidental double-tap or a skipped beat no longer wrecks the reading. And the metronome was rebuilt from a drifting setInterval to lookahead scheduling on the audio clock, so it's sample-accurate and dead-on instead of slowly sliding out of time.

Rebuilt the Tuner's engine for stability and fluidity. Pitch detection moved from a naive O(n²) autocorrelation recomputed on every animation frame to a fast FFT-based McLeod pitch method (~0.4 ms per read, verified accurate to a fraction of a cent), decoupled from the paint loop, median-smoothed so the needle sits still, and paused when the tab is hidden. The DSP now lives in a shared audio-core.js — one fast, correct place for pitch/note math and audio lifecycle that every tool can build on. First step of an ongoing pass to make every interactive feature more stable and more fluid.

🌿 July 4, 2026 🌿

Taught buffer size & latency properly in the Logic Pro reference — what the I/O buffer actually is, the latency-vs-CPU trade-off (drawn), and why you record on a small buffer but mix and especially master on a large one (heavy plugins, and latency never touches the offline bounce). Cross-linked from the mastering prep.

Added stylized Logic Pro interface recreations to the guides — an on-brand Channel EQ (analyzer, curve & band handles), Compressor (gain-reduction meter & knobs), and Loudness Meter (integrated LUFS & true peak), drawn as SVG so they match the site and stay crisp at any size.

New Mastering Guide — the final stage, added as Part 4 of the production path: what mastering is (and isn't), how to prep the mix, the mastering chain in order, gentle EQ & glue, and loudness done right — LUFS targets, true-peak limiting, and why crushing a track flat just gets it turned back down. With metering, references, the dithered bounce, and Logic's tools (Adaptive Limiter, Multipressor, Linear Phase EQ, Loudness Meter, Mastering Assistant).

Tied the production guides into one Learn Production hub — a visual, free path from a raw idea to a finished master: record → arrange → mix → master, each part cross-linked with a next-step trail so you can read straight through or jump to what you need.

New Logic Pro reference — the shortcuts, tools, and features that make music: the main window laid out, the key commands worth memorizing (transport, editing, windows, navigation), the toolbox, and the feature set (Drummer, Flex Time & Pitch, Smart Tempo, quantize, Apple Loops, automation, Track Stacks, Live Loops). The machine behind the production series.

Housekeeping for reliability & reach: old removed pages now 301-redirect instead of returning 404 (clearing a Search Console indexing error), and static assets are cached at Cloudflare's edge for faster repeat loads and less origin traffic.

🌿 July 2, 2026 🌿

New Recording Guide — a visual reference for capturing sound: microphone types and polar patterns (drawn, not just described), mic placement and the proximity effect, stereo miking (XY / ORTF / spaced / Mid-Side), DI & reamping signal flow, how compression actually works, and punch-in / overdub / comping — with Logic Pro X workflow throughout.

New Effects Guide — the companion to Recording: what every audio effect actually does (EQ, dynamics, reverb & delay, modulation, saturation), the order they belong in the signal chain, which effects pair together, and a real method for practicing mixing and training your ears. Fifteen generated diagrams — EQ curves, a reverb impulse, delay trains, comb-filter notches, harmonic spectra — with Logic Pro X workflow throughout.

New Arrangement Guide — how to structure a song and build that structure in Logic Pro X: the anatomy of sections and the energy arc (mapped visually), blocking it out with the Arrangement track, moving whole sections as blocks, arranging energy by adding and subtracting parts, transitions (fills, risers, filter sweeps, cutouts), and a workflow of habits and key commands.

Taught the Song Analyzer to stop tripping YouTube's rate limits: fresh downloads are now deliberately spaced out and capped per hour, so a burst of requests queues into a polite trickle instead of burning the server's download budget — and when the budget runs low, you get an honest message instead of a mysterious failure.

Every song ever analyzed is now a visible Library on the Song Analyzer — one tap replays it instantly with chords, chart, and audio, at zero YouTube cost. The cache was always permanent; now it's browsable.

Added a no-YouTube path to the Song Analyzer: drop any audio file (MP3, M4A, WAV, FLAC) straight onto the page and it's analyzed by the same engine — no downloads, no rate limits, and identical files are recognized and served from cache no matter who uploads them.

Full site hygiene pass: the sitemap now covers all 35 indexable pages (it listed 16 — every chronicle, the Fretboard, Jam Guide, Ear Training, Chord-Tone Trainer, Chord Voicings, and AI pages were invisible to search engines) with real last-modified dates; every page carries a canonical URL; and five tool pages that had no social-preview cards now render properly when shared.

Retired two orphaned early versions of the Song Analyzer that nothing linked to but search engines could still find — both now redirect to the real Song Analyzer instead of competing with it under the same title.

Added a Windows-Media-Player-style spectrum visualizer to the Song Analyzer's play-along panel — now that playback runs through our own audio element, a live frequency display dances with the music (and gracefully sits out on browsers that don't support Web Audio).

Fixed the Song Analyzer failing through the live service while working in every manual test — the root cause was the worker's output piping into journald, whose rate-limiting blocked yt-dlp's progress writes and froze the download. Suppressed the progress output and routed the service's logs to a file instead.

Made the Song Analyzer hold up under simultaneous use: a cross-process concurrency limit now lets overlapping requests from multiple devices queue and run cleanly one at a time instead of dogpiling the box into timeouts.

Sped up a fresh analysis from ~20s to ~12s — the backend now pulls a smaller audio stream, has ffmpeg emit the exact 22 kHz mono the analysis needs, and computes the chroma transform once instead of twice. Repeats of the same song still return instantly from cache.

Made the Song Analyzer's embedded player start loading the moment you hit analyze — in parallel with the analysis instead of only afterward — and switched it to YouTube's no-cookie host to cut down on the embed's "confirm you're not a bot" prompts.

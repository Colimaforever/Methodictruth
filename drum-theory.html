𒀭 The Theory of What You Made

How to Read a Drum Pattern

A bar of 4/4 is divided into 16 steps — four beats, each split into four sixteenth-notes. The four downbeats (steps 1, 5, 9, 13) are the strongest; everything else is weaker to the ear. Where you place hits against that grid is what produces feel.

The Backbeat

Snare on beats 2 and 4 is the backbeat — the single most important convention in Western popular music. It's the head-nod. Move the snare to beat 3 only and the tempo appears to halve (half-time); that's the weight behind trap and doom.

The Kick's Job

A kick on every quarter (four-on-the-floor) is the relentless pulse of house and techno — physical, hypnotic, impossible to sit still to. Kick on 1 and 3 is the rock/pop anchor. Push the kick onto the "ands" and the "e"s and it starts to syncopate — the hip-hop and funk approach, where the groove lives in the spaces between the beats.

Hats & Subdivision

The hi-hat sets the resolution of the groove. Eighth-notes feel steady; sixteenths drive and hurry; an open hat on the offbeat "and" is the disco/house signature. Trap's fluttering rolls are just the hat running at sixteenths and faster.

Swing & Syncopation

Swing drags every second sixteenth slightly late, turning a stiff grid into a loping, human shuffle — the difference between a drum machine and a drummer. Syncopation is accent on the weak positions; a little is groove, a lot is tension. The readout above scores both for whatever you program.

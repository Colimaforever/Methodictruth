Tempo: The Heartbeat of Music

BPM — beats per minute — is the clock that everything in a production locks to. It determines groove, energy, and genre identity. Hip hop lives at 85–95 BPM (slow, heavy, room for syncopation). House sits at 120–130 (the natural pace of walking, which is why it feels so physically intuitive). Drum and bass pushes 170–180 (anxious, kinetic, breakneck). Ambient drifts around 60–80 (meditative, spacious, unhurried). These aren't rules — they're centers of gravity.

Feel vs. Precision

Tap tempo captures something a metronome can't: your perception of the beat. Human time-feel naturally fluctuates — you might rush fills and drag on choruses. That variance is musical. The standard deviation shown above tells you how consistent your internal clock is. A tight ±5ms suggests a steady pocket; wider spread suggests the song itself is pushing and pulling tempo (or you're still locking in).

In production, knowing the exact BPM of a track unlocks everything — syncing delays, quantizing recordings, matching beats for DJ transitions, and setting the grid in your DAW. This tool gives you that number in seconds.

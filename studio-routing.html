Workflow · patchbay · tape

1 · The whole arc

Five stages. Only one is irreversible — which is why it sits where it does, after the arrangement works and before the mix.

  • hardware makes sound
  • nothing recorded yet

  • audio exists now

  • structure the song

4

  • 424 tape · SSL comp
  • RE-202 · springs
  • committed — no undo
  • 3–4 elements only
→
5

Mix

  • balance · automate
  • master · export
The loop you'll actually use: mixing, you hear the drums want more saturation, you re-print just the drums and carry on. Stage 4 ↔ 5, not a one-way line.

Stages 1–3 are free — change anything, as often as you like. Stage 4 commits sound. Stage 5 is free again — and only because the tape comes back as four separate tracks instead of a mix. That is the entire reason for the four tape outputs.

MIDI runs underneath all of it. MOTU 8×8, clock from Logic, R-8 on clock only with MTC/MMC off. Everything stays in time whether or not its audio is being recorded.

3 · The tape workflow

Two passes with a gap between them. Nothing is real-time: you print, then you play back and capture.

PASS 1 — PRINT

Logic

  • the stems you chose
→

Clarett

  • OUT 3–6
→

424 mixer

  • EQ · trim · FX sends
  • commit the character
→

Cassette

  • tracks 1–4

PASS 2 — CAPTURE

Cassette

  • press PLAY
→

Tape out 1–4

  • RCA
  • bypasses the mixer
→

Clarett

  • IN 3–6
→

Logic

  • 4 separate tracks
  • arrange · automate
LINE OUT → SSL → speakers is monitoring, while you work. It is a stereo mix and not the capture path — that is TAPE OUT 1–4. Capturing the machine's own stereo output would freeze every balance decision, which is the thing the four separate outputs exist to prevent.

4 · SSL SiX vs 424 — they are opposites

SSL SiX424 mkIII
Transientstightenssoftens
Top endleaves or liftsrolls off
Dynamicsdeliberate, adjustablea byproduct — what tape gives
Effectmore definedmore cohesive
Speedreal-time — audition freelya pass — print, rewind, capture
You have2 channels + bus comp6 channels
Control level or transients → SSL.
Add texture or weight → 424.
Audition on the SSL. Commit on tape.

Usual order when both are in the chain: Logic → SSL (shape) → 424 (saturate) → tape. Shape it tight, then let tape soften it.

5 · What goes to tape

Four tracks, so you choose. Printing everything flattens the record — the contrast between tape-coloured and clean is what makes the colour read at all.

Usual picks: RD-8 drums (an 808 through tape is the canonical sound), bass, one pad or the VC-340, guitar that already went through the pedals.
Usually leave clean: anything where detail matters more than weight.
